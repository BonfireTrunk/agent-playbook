## Vendor terms

Every vendor that receives the customers' personal data (an AI model provider, hosting, a mail relay, a log store) is a sub-processor. This skill holds no verdict on any vendor: terms differ between vendors, between a vendor's products and plans, and between its models, and they change often. Judge each vendor afresh, from its own legal and docs pages, and record the pages, their dates and versions in the report.

| Point | What to find | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Training | Whether customer data is used to train models, by default and under the contract, and what opts in (feedback, settings) | A CCPA service provider may not use the data for another customer (Reg. 7050(a)(3)); under the GDPR the processor acts only on the controller's documented instructions (28(3)(a)) |
| Retention | How long inputs and outputs are kept by default, and how long content flagged for abuse review is kept | The product's own retention promise cannot be shorter than the vendor's |
| Zero or reduced retention | Whether it exists, whether it needs a signed agreement or approval, and which endpoints, features and models it covers | Per-model or per-feature conditions mean the model or feature chosen can decide whether zero retention is possible at all |
| Human review | Whether vendor staff can read flagged content, or the review is left to the customer | Another party reading personal data, to state in the disclosure |
| Location | Where data rests and where it is processed, and which regions can be chosen for each | A region choice for processing does not always cover storage; a transfer tool may be needed (**LAWYER**) |
| Processing agreement | Whether a DPA applies, how it is incorporated, its version and date | The customer needs the same terms passed down (GDPR 28(4)) |
| Sub-processors | The vendor's own list, its date and its change notice | If the list cannot be read (for example a page that renders only with JavaScript), mark it **UNVERIFIED** and keep it open in the report |
